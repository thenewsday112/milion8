Pickup trucks have become more than work vehicles. Today’s pickups are highly advanced cars with hybrid and electric drive technology like the Rivian R1T and Ford F-150 Lightning. Keeping these modern vehicles safe means moving away from traditional “if it’s not broke, don’t fix it” maintenance routines toward informed, all-around interior protection.

Even if you got that creative power technology running in the back of your truck, there is a mundane set of problems that the interior is facing every single day:

  • Drinks,
  • spilled drinks,
  • tool dust
  • and UV radiation.

Loading your truck for tough hauling, off-roading or one-of-a-kind trips increases the stress on the cabin’s seats. Thus, securing the cab is the top priority to maintain value and structure.

Photo from create.vista.com

Reliable Protection for Seats

Seats are what receive the hardest hit. Regular getting in and out, work clothes and the sun wear down the driver’s side bolster of the seat upholstery in record time. Even universal slip-on covers won’t do, because they shift, bunch up and detract from the classy interior.

It is preferable to find an original replacement for the worn-out factory cloth or leather. For example, premium Ford F-150 OEM seat covers are designed to match the pattern of the original factory. Companies like Auto Champ of Texas offer Ford F-150 seat covers for drivers looking to replace worn upholstery and keep their truck’s interior in good condition. They are fitted in place of the old upholstery, conform to the frame’s foam structure, and do not impair the side-impact airbag’s correct operation. Only replacing the damaged panels preserves the rest of the interior and reduces expenditure.

A Barrier Against Dirt and Water

Floor protection is up next. The factory carpet soaks up winter salt, spilled coffee and moisture like a sponge, turning it into a smelly, moldy mess and, eventually, corrosion.

Swap out the generic cloth floor mats for custom-fit floor liners, designed specifically to fit your vehicle’s floor and constructed of thermoplastic elastomer (TPE). They have high ridges that securely trap water, sand, and mud, and can be easily washed off with a regular garden hose.

Protection from the Heat and UV Rays

Remember the sun. Extreme heat and UV from the sun will cause dashboard plastic to fade and crack over time. The amount of heat generated on the dashboards of modern trucks with mammoth infotainment screens can also take a toll on sensitive electronics.

High-quality ceramic window tinting or even a basic folding reflective sunshade when parked will bring the temperature of the cab down 15-20 degrees. This prevents the dashboard from fading and substantially relieves the pressure on your climate control system. For electric and hybrid pickups, a cooler cab means less energy from your battery goes toward the AC.

Regular Care

All surfaces still require care, even the most durable. Dust and fine sand particles, acting as abrasives, slowly erode the surface of plastic panels and door cards.

Utilize light microfiber cloths and anti-static cleaning agents. The finest formulations not only eliminate grease and dirt but also leave behind a thin film to ward off the accumulation of dust and prevent the plastic from decaying too quickly. Several easy, practical steps now will ensure your pickup looks pristine, comfortable, and attractive on the pre-owned market for years to come.

Introduction

The retail automotive world has seen some major changes during the past few decades. While many people think a car dealership is only for buying a new vehicle, the savvy businesses have realized they need to be much more. In reality, the best dealerships strive to support every stage of your vehicle purchase, from model research to finance, servicing and trade-in help. If you want less guesswork and more clarity, new dealerships can offer a more complete experience than many buyers expect.

Exploring the Full Offerings of Dealerships in Australia

One microcosm of the changes in dealership attitudes and offerings can be found in Australia. Etheridge Ford in Melbourne offers support beyond new vehicle sales, including vehicle selection, finance options and even assistance with your current vehicle.

That wider role matters when you want one point of contact for your vehicle needs. Melbourne Ford Dealerships like Etheridge Ford show how sales, support and after-sales guidance can sit together. The next sections break down where that value really shows and how it affects the business.

New and Used Vehicle Selection:

One clear benefit of a franchise dealer is choice. Instead of chasing separate listings, you can compare new vehicles, demo stock and used options in one visit. Dealers also have the ability to order vehicles that have yet to be built or trade with other new car dealers to get a model or trim that isn’t on the lot. That makes it easier to match price, features and timing.

At Etheridge Ford, buyers can browse a wide variety of vehicles online and in person. The range includes popular models such as the Ford Ranger and Ford Mustang, helping you weigh up a new vehicle against a lower-cost alternative.

  • You can compare new, demo and pre-owned options side-by-side.
  • You get support from staff who work with Fords every day.
  • You can ask about model fit, whether you want a pickup, SUV, car or another type of vehicle.

Used Vehicle Advantages and Dealer Support

Used vehicles matter because they give buyers more entry points. For many buyers, a quality used vehicle can provide a more affordable option, while dealer support may continue after the sale.

Dealers can support used-vehicle buyers with services such as servicing, OEM parts and ongoing customer support. That means your purchase is not treated as a one-off event. Ongoing support, including access to factory-produced parts, adds confidence to a purchase.

Tesla profits drop 43% in Q3

📅 Oct 21, 2026  |  Category: Business  |  Read Source →

Tesla published its third-quarter 2026 financial results this afternoon. Earlier in October, it reported its sales and deliveries for the three-month period, selling fewer cars than in the same quarter in 2025, yet it still beat analyst projections. Now we can see how those sales translate to the bottom line. Tesla brought in $28 billion in revenue in Q3, almost the same as in 2025.

Automotive revenues were $20.6 billion, a 3 percent decrease year over year. Perhaps more troubling is the decline in revenue from solar panels and storage batteries—this line of business had been growing, but for Q3 revenue fell 16 percent year over year to $2.9 billion. Services and other revenues, though, which include parts and Supercharger use, grew by a third to $4.6 billion.

But the company—like the rest of us—has seen its expenses go up. Operating expenses rose by 39 percent to $4.8 billion, dropping the company’s operating margin to just 2.6 percent; in Q3 2025, Tesla’s margin was 5.8 percent. Net profit fell to $787 million, down 43 percent year over year. What’s more, capital expenditures were up by 110 percent to $4.7 billion, and free cash flow was down 62 percent to $1.5 billion.
