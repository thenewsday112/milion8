A Personal Perspective on the Western Automotive Journalists’ Best of the Bay 2026

[Background: Once a year Western Automotive Journalists (WAJ) invites automakers to bring some of their best new models to the San Francisco Bay Area and WAJ members spend a day-and-a-half driving each and then reporting their findings as the Best of the Bay. This year’s eclectic group of a dozen cars are the subject of long-time WAJ member Andrew Frankl’s overview below. Clean Fleet Report will have additional reporting on the event soon. Another WAJ member, Michael Hagerty, also offers his perspective here.]

The annual Best of Bay event is always the highlight of my automotive year. Just like in previous years, 2026 was another great one!

Surprise—a McLaren!

This year we had two wonderful surprises—a McLaren Artura and Maserati’s Grecale Modena V6. What Nolan Gray of McLaren could not have known was that Bruce McLaren, founder of the company, and I had been friends in the 1960s. As we all know, he unfortunately lost his life testing a car back in 1970. The motor racing world lost a great driver and designer. I am just very pleased that the name is very much alive. In fact, the 2025 F1 world champion drove a car bearing Bruce’s name.

The Artura was new to me, but I think Nolan was a little bit surprised when I showed him a video of me driving a McLaren back in 2011 on that famous race track where Jeremy Clarkson and his friends did all their crazy stunts. The color hasn’t changed, I am pleased to say, and the Artura is OK now. I am saying now, because early on it had a myriad of problems, mainly electrical. Some prices dropped from $300 to $200,000 dollars in a year. For McLaren to climb back from such steep depreciation, it had to get all the gremlins sorted. Judging by the car I had a chance to drive, they did. The problem is to get the message across.

The V6 engine is superb and sounds just as good as a Ferrari or a Maserati. The handling has been tweaked by current world champion Lando Norris. No surprises here, Michael Schumacher used to fine-tune Ferraris while Honda’s NSX was as brilliant as it was thanks to the unforgettable Ayrton Senna. With all the AI money flooding into San Francisco the question is—should you buy one? Well, try it against the Ferrari GTB and decide. A first world problem if ever there was one.

The Maserati Grecale – Great sound and history, questionable value

Maserati Grecale

The Maserati Grecale also came with a serious pedigree. More by luck than judgement, I was at the factory in Modena last year just as they were celebrating 100 years of motorsport. Over the years they have had huge success in autoracing, culminating in Juan Manuel Fangio’s F1 victory in the priceless 250F. To this day his victory at the Nurburgring in 1957, overcoming a 50-second pit stop, is truly a fairytale. I had the honor of meeting him in 1993–unforgettable.

Wish I could say the same about the Grecale. It is all right, but no more. The best bit is the engine, that wonderful sonorous V6 which you will also find in Ferraris. The price for such a famous marque is an attractive $90,000, but regrettably Maseratis have a history of very low second-hand value which, just like McLaren, they are trying hard to overcome.

Lexus ES 500e Premium

My pick for the best, but with work to do

When you compare the Maserati to the 2026 Lexus ES 500e Premium, it is no contest. The Lexus is $30,000 cheaper. OK, it is electric and does not have a racing heritage, but it incredibly comfortable. If you add the legendary reputation that Lexus has, you will skip the Maserati and several other rivals.

I am a little bit disappointed in the 272-mile-range. BMW’s new iX3, admittedly slightly smaller, is good for something like 400 miles. So there is work to do on that score. Certainly there is no need to improve the optional brilliant 17-speaker, 1,800-watt Mark Levinson audio system. Acceleration (0-60 mph) is 5.1 seconds, which is good for a large car. My only beef is the range. Imagine in January, you are trying to get to Incline Village near Lake Tahoe with the heater and the sound system full on and four people on board. Range anxiety will rear its ugly head.

Knowing Lexus, I am sure they are working on the problem.

Mazda CX-30 2.5 Turbo Aire Edition

The Mazda CX-30 – Voted best overall

Many of my colleagues raved about the 2026 Mazda’s CX-30 2.5 Turbo Aire Edition. It was voted overall winner of the 2026 Best of the Bay Award! I agree with most of the conclusions, but I still think it could use a little more horsepower. Having said that, when it comes to value for money you simply cannot beat it. At $37,000 with options and delivery charges, it really is a bargain.

One major complaint—a very slippery steering wheel. Mazda tells me it is leather-wrapped, but it is very, very slippery. Fuel consumption at 25 mpg (EPA combined) is not stunning, but with a bit of judicious driving you should be able to get 30 miles per gallon. If I were in the market for a reasonably priced, exceptionally well-equipped automobile, the CX-30 would definitely be on my shortlist. The interior is truly exceptional! Well done, Mazda

Honda Prelude/Hyundai Palisade Calligraphy

The Honda Prelude takes one look at you and says—take me to the next corner! Indeed, you look at this $42,000 mini-rocket and you know it ancestry. The race track, or at the very least, some twisty roads the likes of which you will find in West Marin. It has a 2.0-liter direct injection engine, Brembo front brake calipers, paddle shifts—the works. The Prelude is a little bit too small for me, and I have retired from being a boy racer, but I can definitely see the appeal of this very attractive little machine. Personally, I prefer the Miata. For me, open air motoring in that little gem is unparalleled.

The Hyundai Palisade Calligraphy AWD makes Range Rover and other luxury SUVs look silly. I don’t know how they do it. How they can sell this amazingly luxurious product for under $60,000 is a mystery to me. Interestingly, more of my colleagues prefer the four-cylinder hybrid to the V6, but neither engine is powerful enough to charge up to Tahoe with the large family occupying all seats in the second and third rows. While mechanically the Hyundai is not getting any Oscars, the interior certainly does! So, come on Hyundai, find a little bit more oomph and better fuel economy to reach the top of the class not just in the cabin, but under the bonnet as well.

The Best of the Bay trophies – Photo by Maurice Liang

You might be interested to know how the WAJ judges—all highly qualified automotive journalists—voted of the Best of the Bay.

  • Best Overall + Best Compact SUV: 2026 Mazda CX-30 2.5 Turbo Aire Edition
  • Best Car (and for me first overall): the 2026 Lexus ES 500e Premium
  • Best EV: 2027 Kia EV3 GT-Line AWD
  • Best Luxury SUV: 2026 Hyundai Palisade Calligraphy AWD
  • Best Supercar: 2026 McLaren Artura

As for the rest, there were no losers. Automotive engineering in improving leaps and bounds.

Bring on Best of the Bay 2027!

During most of automotive history, you could only see a car’s badge clearly in daylight. They are mostly made of chrome, enamel and polished plastic. Therefore, once night falls, the badge will disappear in the darkness. Electric vehicles are changing that. Since they have no engine that needs a large radiator opening, the front of an EV has become a smooth, flat panel, and automakers are filling it with light.

Cadillac Lyriq – Photo by Michael Coates

Cadillac began this transition early. The Lyriq, the brand’s first all-electric model, has a black-and-white version of the Cadillac crest which can light up from behind. The Escalade IQ carried the idea to a much larger front-end design. In detail, an illuminated crest, a partly lit grille and vertical LED columns define the characteristics of its front fascia.

Audi has been more cautious in this regard, partly because of regulation. The Q8 E-tron marks the debut of a flat, chrome-free version of the “Four Rings” emblem. Also, Audi stated that it will add indirect grille lighting to frame the logo. The A6 E-tron then became the first Audi model with illuminated four-ring at the rear. Audi lighting engineers have said that step was possible only after new European rules came into effect in January 2023.

Why Light the Logo?

In fact, there are practical reasons behind the trend. EVs are very quiet when in motion, and their side profiles often look quite similar. Therefore, an illuminated badge gives a brand a highly recognizable “signature” at night. Moreover, LED lighting uses very little energy, so this design has almost no impact on a vehicle’s driving range. As headlights shrink into thin strips, the badge has become one of the few large surfaces where a brand can make a statement.

The Look Moves into the Aftermarket

Not every owner drives a new EV, but this look has spread to older and gas-powered vehicles. People can use some aftermarket kits to replace the factory badge with an LED unit that matches the original shape. An Audi light-up emblem, for example, is specially designed to match the dimensions of a specific model’s grille badge. Therefore, you don’t need to cut the grille any more.

Cadillac owners have similar options. A light-up Cadillac emblem is usually designed to follow the crest’s outline. Also, it can be wired to light up with the parking lights or when a door is opened.

What to Check Before Installing

  • Local lighting rules. In most U.S. states and Canadian provinces, there are restrictions for forward-facing red lights and flashing lights. So you had better check local regulations before you choose a color or a dynamic pattern.
  • Sensors behind the badge. Some newer models, including the Audi Q4 E-tron, are equipped with driver-assistance sensors behind the front badge. If a replacement badge blocks the radar, it may affect the adaptive cruise control system.
  • Wiring. Plug-and-play kits are easier to reverse than hardwired ones. A proper fuse can protect the 12-volt system, which is responsible for powering lights and accessories in electric vehicles.
  • Weather sealing. A badge is exposed to rain, road salt and car-wash spray. Therefore, the waterproof rating is really important.

The badge has always an iconic feature of cars. In the electric era, it is also becoming a light source, which owners of older vehicles can also enjoy now.

Cadillac Escalade IQ review: Huge battery, huge range, huge SUV

📅 Oct 28, 2026  |  Category: Other  |  Read Source →

“Please have at least 125 miles of range left when we pick it up.” That request from the people who look after the Cadillac press cars wasn’t going to be hard to honor. The electric Escalade IQ arrived with an indicated range of 440 miles (708 km), and with a full week of work to squeeze in, getting anywhere close to needing to recharge it seemed unlikely. With more than 200 kWh of lithium-ion battery between its axles, range is one thing the big electric Caddy needn’t worry about.

Designed and debuted during a time of greater EV optimism, the Escalade is Cadillac’s statement that swapping out a V8 for a pair of powerful electric motors is no compromise to its ambitions, even if the brand’s future will now include a mix of electric and combustion cars beyond 2030. It shares a battery pack and much else with other full-size GM EVs we’ve tested: the Chevrolet Silverado EV, the GMC Sierra EV, and of course the divisive Hummer EVs. And like those trucks and SUVs, the Escalade is large and heavy—just small enough to fit into a standard parking space, with a curb weight sufficient to push it into the Class 3 truck category.

So, the side mirrors are flat, and there’s no formal EPA range—Cadillac says it achieves 460 miles (740 km) on the EPA test cycle—but there are plenty of airbags, even though the 9,046 lb (4,103 kg) curb weight does not require them. Yes, you did read that right, it does weigh 4 tons. And yes, the electric Escalade does weigh a third more than the last gas-powered one we tested.

Ford simplifies using your EV as a home battery with the $30K Fathom

📅 Oct 27, 2026  |  Category: Technology  |  Read Source →

This morning, Ford revealed that buyers of the forthcoming Fathom—the company's sub-$30,000 electric pickup truck due next year—will get more than just a truck. Until now, vehicle-to-home technology—like the setup Ford developed for the now-discontinued F-150 Lightning—takes DC power from the electric vehicle and runs it through an inverter built into the wallbox to turn it into AC the house can use. But with the Fathom, that inverter is built into the truck from the start. So every Fathom will be able to act as a backup battery for your home.

It’s typical: You wait a decade for a cheap electric truck, and then two come along almost at once. And they’re two radically different products from two radically different automakers that I think will reveal a few preferences from the EV-buying public. One is the Blank Slate, from a brand new startup offering a bare-bones two-door that capitalizes on features the Internet says it wants, like no onboard modem, no touchscreens, and no infotainment system.

The other is the result of one of Detroit’s big three taking a long, hard look at Chinese EVs and asking itself, “How do we make a car that cheap?” Although the Ford Fathom will cost $5,000 more than a Slate, that money does buy an extra pair of doors, modern conveniences like an infotainment system, CarPlay, electric windows, and a vast dealership network to service the car. That may be helpful given how many recalls Ford issues each year.
