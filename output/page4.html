GM to pay $220 million after selling customer Smart Driver data

📅 Oct 30, 2026  |  Category: Technology  |  Read Source →

General Motors marketed its Smart Driver system as a way for parents to monitor teen drivers or for adults to track their driving behavior. But in 2024, it emerged that GM had been sharing that data with brokers. Those brokers then sold the data to insurance companies, and some drivers found that their insurance became more expensive as a result. To make matters worse, many of those drivers claimed they had no knowledge of ever agreeing to share such data for such purposes.

Last year, GM and its OnStar subsidiary entered into a settlement with the FTC, prohibiting them from sharing some kinds of driver data with third parties. The settlement was finalized this January. That agreement banned the two companies from sharing geolocation data or driver behavior data for five years. At the time, GM also discontinued Smart Driver across its entire range.

Now the automaker has reached terms with the District of Columbia and 35 states after their attorneys general joined together to sue GM as well.

2027 Nissan Rogue Hybrid: Drives like an EV, but there's no plug involved

📅 Oct 30, 2026  |  Category: Technology  |  Read Source →
Nissan provided flights from Washington, DC, to Santa Barbara, California, and accommodation so Ars could drive the Rogue Hybrid. Ars does not accept paid editorial content.

Nissan has a new version of its bestselling Rogue SUV, and it’s launching the model here with the latest version of its e-Power hybrid system, which Ars tested in February. Most hybrid cars you can buy today are parallel hybrids, which means that they have an internal combustion engine and an electric motor, both of which drive some or all of the wheels—everything works in parallel. But there’s another approach. Instead of connecting the engine to the wheels via a transmission, it can act as a generator and charge the traction battery, which in turn powers the electric motor. Since the engine and motor work in series, this is known as a series hybrid.

That’s mostly how the Chevrolet Volt worked. As does Honda’s hybrid CR-V. Well, mostly; engineers worked out it was more efficient for those cars' gasoline engines to directly drive the wheels at highway speeds, something that to this day makes a certain subset of hybrid car enthusiasts irrationally angry. If you know anyone like that in your life, send them to wherever the nearest Nissan dealership is, because the 2027 Rogue Hybrid is exactly what they’ve been asking for.

Series hybrids

Under the hood, with its crisp shut line and slim horizontal lights that give me something of a Range Rover vibe, there’s a 1.5 L, three-cylinder turbocharged direct-injection engine that generates 148 hp (110 kW). It only revs to 5,200 rpm because it’s optimized to charge the battery, not cope with the various loads of an engine connected to a gearbox and the wheels. That also allows the engine to operate at a rather impressive 42 percent thermal efficiency—we would need to look at Formula 1 engines to find higher.

Coming away from a truck crash with a broken bone, spine or traumatic brain injury might require months of treatment. A few victims might end up living with lifelong restrictions. To get a handle on the next steps after an injury accident, it’s recommended to get in touch with a truck accident attorney, like the Hollis Law Firm in Overland Park, Kansas.

Recovery Does Not Always Follow a Straight Line

Some injuries don’t resolve after a trip to the emergency room. There may be follow-up visits, physical therapy, prescriptions, specialists and recovery. It is also possible for a person to experience a slow and steady improvement in symptoms, or to have new challenges crop up after they try to resume their life.

This is relevant to injury law because it’s clear that the fallout from an incident can last well beyond the time when the victim got taken to the hospital. Compensation needs to account for ongoing medical needs and other shifts in a person’s capacity to work, take care of themselves and live their life.

The Financial Pressure Can Build Quickly

Medical expenses aren’t the only costs. A person who is recovering from a crash may be out of work and need assistance around the home. They may also face the added expense of finding alternate transportation while their car is being repaired.

For families, these costs can be difficult as an injured person tries to concentrate on healing. Personal injury law is a way to help account for those losses when they are due to someone else’s negligence.

Some Losses Are Harder to Measure

Not every consequence is reflected on a receipt. Someone may lose time with their family, may not feel up to doing the things they love, may experience pain and other physical symptoms, may have trouble sleeping or moving about or may become dependent on someone else as a result of an injury. Pain and emotional suffering also affect people’s quality of life.

The law recognizes that an injury has consequences that are both personal and financial, and that an injured person is entitled to seek compensation for their losses. The specific categories of losses that a person may recover will depend on the facts of the case and the law that applies.

Legal Protections Can Support the Recovery Process

The point of personal injury law isn’t to change what happened. It doesn’t give accident victims a miracle that eliminates the effects of another party’s actions. But it does give a legal system for addressing those effects, and sometimes that can provide a way to pursue compensation for resulting damages while accountability is determined under appropriate authorities.

If you, or someone you care about, is healing from a truck accident, it can be easier when you understand some of the rights that protect individuals in those situations. It helps to know that medical bills, lost wages and other effects can be addressed with rules behind them, rather than layered on top of the other problems.
